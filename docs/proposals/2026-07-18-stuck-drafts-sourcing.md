# Source verification for stuck drafts #465, #466, and #467

Verified on 2026-07-18. This memo is limited to citation finding and claim
boundaries; it does not rewrite or distill the draft items. Every URL below was
found as a real, live page. Qualifications call out wording that the linked
source does **not** establish.

## Item 1 — Wedding permit payment (#465)

### Verified sources

1. **Mayor Mamdani's statement that Swift paid more than $160,000 for the
   permit and event response**

   `sourceUrl: "https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/07/transcript--mayor-mamdani-announces-landmark--click-to-cancel--c"`

   Supports: The official July 10 press-conference transcript records a
   reporter asking about police overtime and Mamdani saying Swift had already
   paid the permit cost, which was over $160,000, "for that event and for the
   response to that event."

   Reliability: **Primary official source** — a City of New York Mayor's Office
   transcript, not entertainment-media aggregation.

2. **ABC News reporting on the permit payment, NYPD deployment, and $26
   million in donations (including an NYC-serving charity)**

   `sourceUrl: "https://abcnews.com/amp/GMA/Culture/taylor-swift-paid-new-york-city-160k-wedding/story?id=134655199"`

   Supports: ABC reports (a) the more-than-$160,000 permit payment and the
   mayor's statement; (b) that the NYPD deployed **dozens of officers** to
   manage closures and traffic around Madison Square Garden; and (c) that a
   Swift representative confirmed Swift and Kelce donated $26 million to
   several charities before the wedding. ABC specifically names Answer The
   Call, an NYC organization supporting families of police officers and
   firefighters killed in the line of duty.

   Reliability: **Major national news outlet** — ABC News/GMA, citing the
   mayor, the NYPD, and Swift's representative for the respective facts.

3. **Corroboration for a precise deployment count of 135 officers**

   `sourceUrl: "https://www.cbsnews.com/news/taylor-swift-travis-kelce-wedding-day-madison-square-garden-nyc/"`

   Supports: CBS reports that **135 officers were assigned around the venue on
   Friday**, attributing the number to law-enforcement sources. Use this source,
   not the ABC article, if the draft needs that exact count.

   Reliability: **Major national news outlet**, although the precise count is
   attributed to unnamed law-enforcement sources rather than an on-record NYPD
   statement.

4. **Independent corroboration of the $26 million donation and NYC recipients**

   `sourceUrl: "https://apnews.com/article/taylor-swift-travis-kelce-charity-wedding-63185de97849e25fba78e79e2ec710a4"`

   Supports: AP reports $26 million distributed among 20 charities, based on
   Swift's publicist, and says nine were based in New York, including Food Bank
   For NYC, City Harvest, and Musical Mentors.

   Reliability: **Major wire service** — Associated Press, citing Swift's
   publicist and identifying named beneficiary organizations.

### Claim limits / sources not found

- All three requested core claims have live sources.
- ABC's verified wording is only **"dozens of officers"**; ABC does not give a
  precise officer count in the linked article. The separate CBS report gives
  135.
- The official transcript says the permit payment covered the event and its
  response, after a question about police overtime. It does **not** itemize the
  $160,000 or unambiguously say that this amount reimbursed every dollar of
  NYPD overtime. Do not strengthen the claim to "all police overtime was paid"
  based on this transcript alone.

## Item 2 — Post-wedding public appearance (#466)

### Verified sources

1. **PEOPLE article with the July 10 appearance and exact Markarian dress ID**

   `sourceUrl: "https://people.com/taylor-swift-and-travis-kelce-attend-juju-smith-schuster-wedding-1-week-after-msg-nuptials-12011541"`

   Supports: PEOPLE reports that Swift and Kelce attended JuJu
   Smith-Schuster and Laura Kruk's wedding on Friday, July 10, one week after
   their own wedding. It publishes what it labels the first pictures of the
   couple since their MSG wedding and identifies Swift's dress as Markarian's
   **$4,115 Laila floral brocade gown**.

   Reliability: **Major celebrity/entertainment outlet** — PEOPLE; the article
   includes event photographs and links the identified gown to a retailer.

2. **Other-outlet coverage of the first post-wedding photographs**

   `sourceUrl: "https://www.tmz.com/2026/07/10/taylor-swift-and-travis-kelce-seen-first-time-since-wedding/"`

   Supports: TMZ's headline calls these the first photos of Swift and Kelce
   since their wedding, and its July 10 report says it obtained photographs of
   the newlyweds at Smith-Schuster and Kruk's wedding.

   Reliability: **Entertainment tabloid/direct photo publisher** — weaker than
   a wire service for general factual claims, but directly relevant as the
   outlet publishing the photographs it describes.

### Claim limits / sources not found

- Both the July 10 attendance and the Laila gown identification have live
  sources.
- The sources support **first published/photographed sighting since the July 3
  wedding**. They cannot prove that no earlier private or unphotographed outing
  occurred. Keep the claim framed as the first photographed public appearance,
  not literally the couple's first time outside after marrying.

## Item 3 — “I Knew It, I Knew You” / *Toy Story 5* Oscar discussion (#467)

### Verified sources

1. **Lin-Manuel Miranda's comments about Swift writing honestly for a
   character**

   `sourceUrl: "https://nz.news.yahoo.com/lin-manuel-miranda-praises-taylor-154403053.html"`

   Supports: This live Yahoo syndication of a PEOPLE interview reports Miranda
   saying he loved the song and that film-songwriters focus on making their
   writing for characters feel honest and true; he said Swift did a wonderful
   job of that in *Toy Story 5*. The article separately identifies Jessie as
   the character who partly inspired the song.

   Reliability: **Major news portal carrying a syndicated PEOPLE interview** —
   the remarks are presented as a direct PEOPLE red-carpet interview, but this
   is a Yahoo-hosted republication rather than a primary PEOPLE.com URL.

2. **Variety discussion of Best Original Song prospects**

   `sourceUrl: "https://variety.com/2026/music/columns/taylor-swift-oscar-chances-best-song-toy-story-i-knew-it-1236784338/"`

   `accessibleMirrorUrl: "https://au.variety.com/2026/music/news/taylor-swift-oscar-chances-best-song-toy-story-i-knew-it-37790/"`

   Supports: Chris Willman's Variety analysis is explicitly about whether “I
   Knew It, I Knew You” could earn Swift her first Oscar. It discusses the
   Academy music branch's future shortlist and nomination voting, the song's
   strengths, and possible obstacles.

   Reliability: **Entertainment-industry trade analysis** — the first URL is
   the original Variety US column (paywalled); Variety Australia/New Zealand
   hosts the accessible authorized version and identifies it as coming from
   Variety US. It is credible awards commentary, not an Academy announcement or
   proof that the song has already been submitted.

3. **Official Academy submission deadline**

   `sourceUrl: "https://submissions.oscars.org/default.aspx"`

   Supports: The Academy's live 99th Academy Awards submissions page lists the
   Original Song due date as **October 14, 2026, at 5:00 p.m. PT**.

   Reliability: **Primary official source** — the Academy of Motion Picture
   Arts and Sciences' own submissions portal.

4. **Official Academy rules confirming the same deadline**

   `sourceUrl: "https://www.oscars.org/sites/oscars/files/2026-05/99th_oscars_complete_rules.pdf"`

   Supports: Rule 19, section III(F), says Original Song submissions are due by
   5:00 p.m. PT on Wednesday, October 14, 2026.

   Reliability: **Primary official source** — the Academy's complete rules for
   the 99th Academy Awards.

### Claim limits / sources not found

- The October 14, 2026 date is **confirmed**, with the useful precision that
  the deadline is 5:00 p.m. Pacific Time.
- The Variety piece supports describing the song as an early possible Oscar
  contender or as generating Oscar discussion. It does **not** establish
  official contender status or confirm that Disney/Pixar has submitted it.
- I found **no live source in which Miranda explicitly contrasts the song with
  “just a promotional single.”** His published comment is about writing
  honestly and truthfully for characters. Jessie is named by the article's
  surrounding context, not by Miranda in the quoted remarks. The stronger
  “rather than just a promotional single” formulation is therefore unsupported
  and should not be attributed to him.
- I did not locate a live primary PEOPLE.com version of the Miranda interview.
  The Yahoo URL above is a live syndicated copy carrying the PEOPLE byline and
  interview text; no plausible-looking PEOPLE URL has been invented here.
